# Content containers distilled from 小黄本课程

Read this reference when a raw idea needs the right content format, or when writing an opinion, process, knowledge, or story video. It distills a user-provided 36-slide course deck; it does not reproduce the course text or override the user's request.

## Start with the viewing reason

A useful content position combines at least one of these audience rewards:

- utility: helps the owner make or execute a better decision;
- curiosity: reveals a result, mechanism, contrast, or unfamiliar overseas reality;
- resonance: accurately names a pressure, mistake, frustration, or ambition the owner recognizes.

Prefer two rewards in one piece. For example, a surprising overseas observation earns curiosity, while a decision rule earns utility. Do not confuse a service introduction with a viewing reason.

Use four flexible content containers rather than a mandatory sequence:

1. opinion: take a defensible position on a conflict, misconception, or decision;
2. process: let viewers watch real work unfold;
3. knowledge: solve, prevent, compare, explain, or recommend;
4. story: use people, pressure, choices, turns, and consequences to carry a judgment.

Choose the container that best expresses the seed. Hybrid formats are allowed, but one must lead.

## Make language visible and forceful

- Replace abstract claims with observable behavior, a scene, a decision, or a consequence.
- Use an analogy only when it makes the mechanism easier to picture; avoid decorative metaphors.
- Use contrast—before/after, expected/actual, cheap/expensive, fast/slow, owner/consumer, China/local market—to create force without shouting.
- Prefer concrete verbs and numbers with a verified basis. Avoid slogan stacks and artificial dialect.

## Opinion container

Find a tension involving an industry, product, operator, customer, market choice, common belief, or current event. State one clear judgment and support it.

Useful evidence patterns:

- cost-benefit: what the owner gains or loses by choosing this path;
- two-sided comparison: when the claim is true and when it fails;
- stakeholder perspectives: owner, distributor, local customer, creator, regulator, or partner;
- time horizon: what looks sensible today but creates a later constraint;
- case evidence: a verified experience that proves or limits the judgment.

For OLIF, do not manufacture camps merely to create comments. Do not use personal attacks, blame-shifting, humiliation, or claims about motives without evidence. A controversial opening is valuable only when the body can defend it.

## Process container

Suitable material includes market research, factory visits, overseas partner calls, creator selection, website reviews, campaign execution, event preparation, channel visits, media coordination, and post-campaign analysis.

Build the sequence as:

> consequential starting condition → a few decisive steps → obstacles or discoveries → visible result or next decision

The hook belongs in the starting condition: why this work matters, what could go wrong, what is being tested, or what result is uncertain. Each included step should reveal professional judgment, not merely prove that the team was busy.

Useful expectation devices for this account include budget constraints, time limits, a disagreement, a verification task, an unknown result, or a counterintuitive local rule. Avoid sexual bait, staged charity, pranks, fake obstruction, and arbitrary challenges that damage B2B trust.

## Knowledge container

A knowledge piece should do at least one job:

- improve an outcome;
- solve a concrete problem;
- prevent a costly mistake;
- reduce money, time, or coordination cost;
- give the viewer a useful explanation worth repeating.

Choose one of four structures:

- problem solving: recognizable situation → consequence → executable response;
- recommendation: identify the audience → promise a benefit → rank recommendations by audience value;
- mechanism or risk reveal: observable issue → how the system or incentive works → how to respond;
- case-led lesson: concise case → extracted rule → how the viewer applies or limits it.

Make the first useful payoff arrive early. “Low action cost” means the viewer can take a sensible next step; it does not mean pretending a complex market-entry problem has a three-step universal answer.

## Story container

Use one of three sources:

- Olif's own earned result or hard lesson;
- a client's or partner's outcome, with permission and factual boundaries;
- an ordinary person's principled action under pressure.

A reliable arc is:

> real starting pressure → choice → complication or discovery → response → consequence → earned insight

One turn is enough if it is true. Never insert extra disasters, betrayals, saviors, or miracle outcomes to mimic a dramatic template. In client stories, the client remains the protagonist; Olif can be the strategist, connector, or operator without claiming sole credit.

## Adapt high-attention elements safely

The course proposes recurring attention elements. For OLIF, translate them into credible B2B forms:

- cost: the budget, wasted spend, opportunity cost, or cheap-looking decision that becomes expensive;
- people: a sharply defined owner, buyer, distributor, worker, local partner, or consumer;
- unusual: a real market rule, behavior, or execution detail that violates Chinese assumptions;
- worst case: the most damaging mistake, weak plan, or failed route, with evidence;
- memory and change: how a market, channel, or generation of owners used to operate versus now;
- category leader: what leading brands or operators do differently, without unsupported ranking claims.

Do not use sexualization, sensational labels, or a fake “number one” claim as generic traffic shortcuts.

## OLIF format map

- A raw judgment about a market or owner mistake usually becomes an opinion or knowledge piece.
- A real day of calls, visits, selection, negotiation, or delivery usually becomes a process piece.
- A client result becomes a knowledge-led case when the decision rule matters most, and a story when the human pressure and choices matter most.
- A current event becomes an opinion piece only if Olif has a distinct, useful lens; otherwise treat it as a research lead, not content.

Before drafting, silently check: Why would an owner watch this now? Which single container leads? What is the earliest payoff? What evidence makes this specifically OLIF's content? What would become false or cheesy if dramatized?
