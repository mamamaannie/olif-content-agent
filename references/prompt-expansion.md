# Prompt expansion protocol

Read this reference when the user provides an incomplete seed, mixes several intentions, supplies a very specific direction without production details, or asks broadly for hotspots or “anything about going overseas”.

## Silent editorial brief

Convert the user's message into an internal brief before deciding or drafting. Preserve every explicit fact, choice, request, prohibition, link, and intended outcome. Infer only the routine fields the user should not need to prepare:

- what the user is actually asking the editor to accomplish;
- which Chinese owner should care and what money, time, order, credibility, opportunity, or decision is at stake;
- the strongest defensible conflict or unanswered question, and whether it will use factual or creative mode;
- traffic, trust, or conversion;
- opinion, knowledge, process, or story;
- whether current research, a case, a benchmark, or a factual confirmation is needed;
- the central claim, proof burden, appropriate length, and useful deliverable.

Keep the brief internal unless the user asks to see the enriched prompt. Do not turn it into a questionnaire or recite assumptions that do not affect the answer.

## Adapt to the input

### Specific direction

Preserve the chosen topic, subject, event, audience, tone, and constraints. Add missing stakes, conflict, evidence, format, and delivery details. Replace the direction only when it is factually unsafe, too weak to pay off, or contradicted by evidence; explain that judgment briefly.

### Broad hotspot request

Research current signals and privately compare candidate topics by audience stake, freshness, verifiability, Olif's credible advantage, and room for an original judgment. Select one strongest direction for the user. Do not make the user choose from a routine list of trends or hooks.

### Raw thought, story, or complaint

Find the public problem inside the material. Keep private experience as evidence after the audience stake is clear. When traffic and account heat are primary, missing personal facts should trigger a fictional, composite, or dramatized version rather than a refusal or routine questionnaire; label that choice to the user backstage. Ask at most one or two questions only when the user needs the story treated as factual evidence and truth, permission, result attribution, or the central angle would materially change.

### Link, news, or benchmark work

Verify the underlying event and distinguish what is fact, source claim, visible performance, and interpretation. Extract reusable variables rather than language. Follow the originality and attribution gate in [research-and-remix.md](research-and-remix.md).

### Existing draft

Preserve any line, claim, or structure the user explicitly wants kept. Diagnose the largest bottleneck before rewriting; do not polish a weak premise into smoother but still ineffective copy.

### Publication data or user revisions

Treat retention, completion, interactions, inquiries, audience profile, and the user's own rewrites as calibration evidence. Diagnose them against the post's intended job and update future choices only from repeated or clearly meaningful signals.

## Question threshold

Proceed with clearly labeled assumptions or a backstage-labeled creative mode when uncertainty is low-risk and reversible. Ask no more than one or two targeted questions when the missing answer affects factual safety, private-case permission, material attribution, a promised result, or the main creative direction. Even then, give a provisional judgment or a high-potential fictional or composite draft when useful rather than withholding the work.

The user's prompt only needs to contain what only they know. The skill supplies the editorial structure, research plan, creative judgment, and output format.
